# Exercise design for young ESL learners — research notes (2026-10-19)

Short notes behind the worksheet, dictation and unit-test changes. Greek-L1 classes, A–E Senior (about ages 8–13, Pre-A1 to B1).

## What the evidence says

| Finding | Source | What we changed |
|---|---|---|
| **Cambridge YLE** (Starters / Movers / Flyers) tests all four skills with short, picture-led tasks: *look and read, write yes/no about a picture, look and write the word, read and choose, listen and draw lines / tick the box / colour*, a short speaking interview with picture prompts. Every part opens with a worked **example**. Item counts are small (5–7 per part). | [Cambridge YLE handbook](https://assets.cambridgeenglish.org/network-services/yle-handbook-for-teachers.pdf), [2018 update](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/images/419671-young-learners-2018-exam-update.pdf), [overview handout](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/425863-yle-2018-update-overview-handout-final-spreads.pdf), [Flyers candidate info](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/es/Images/24783-flyers-information-for-candidates-en-.pdf) | The unit **test** uses YLE-style task types per skill, each section with an example line (item 0), 5–8 items, plain imperative rubrics ("Look and read. Write yes or no."). The picture exercise (D25) became YLE "look and write" with real illustrations. |
| **Retrieval beats recognition and re-reading**; spaced, low-stakes retrieval improves long-term retention of vocabulary, also with primary pupils (Goossens et al.). The best spacing schedule is still debated. | [Learning Scientists summary](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2017/7/20-1), [spaced repetition review (EFL/ESL)](https://riset.unisma.ac.id/index.php/JREALL/article/view/25562), [classroom spaced retrieval poster](https://www.wisconsin.edu/opid/download/wtfs_reports/wtfs_17-18/2017-18-WTFS-Jacobson-Poster.pdf) | Tests and dictations lean on **recall** (write the word, complete the sentence) over pure recognition; the test builder mixes recognition → recall within each skill. Answer keys are balanced so guessing doesn't pay. |
| **Involvement load** (need + search + evaluation): tasks where learners must decide whether a word fits a context (evaluation) and produce it themselves give better retention than gloss-reading. | [Laufer & Hulstijn 2001, summaries](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/5C5984B326F37FBF3A3DDA3C0EB2996C), [involvement-load studies](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/4596425.pdf) | Gap-fills with a word bank of **the exact forms needed**, sentence completion in a new context (never the table's collocation), T/F statements that require understanding, and a guided writing task in the test. |
| **Pictures + explaining the link** deepen processing; matching on one visual cue alone is shallow (students confuse similar pictures). | [Colorín Colorado: picture-word strategy](https://www.colorincolorado.org/node/68553), [lexical scaffolding study](https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-00801053v1/file/Lexical_Scaffolding.pdf) | Picture items use the app's own illustrations (no ambiguous emoji) plus a short clue that separates look-alike pictures. |
| **Dictation variants** keep dictation communicative: *running dictation* (pairs: runner memorises chunks from a wall text, writer writes), *dictogloss* (short text read twice at normal speed, pairs reconstruct meaning and form), *gapped* (students fill the missing words while listening), *picture dictation* (listen and draw / listen and write the word for a picture). A dictogloss text is short and connected. | [busyteacher: 8 ways to use dictation](https://busyteacher.org/25011-8-great-ways-to-use-dictation-with-esl-students.html), [running dictation (Victoria DET, after British Council)](https://www.education.vic.gov.au/Documents/school/teachers/teachingresources/diversity/eal/continuum/rundictation.pdf), [dictation re-imagined (NGL)](https://infocus.eltngl.com/2017/07/20/dictation-re-imagined/), [10 creative dictation activities](https://eslgames.com/esl-dictation-activities/) | The new **Dictation** maker has five styles (classic, gapped, running, dictogloss, picture), with length, count, difficulty and grammar controls, and level-based defaults. |
| **Scaffolded writing** for young learners: model text → sentence frames → word bank → short independent writing (YLE Flyers writes 20+ words from pictures). | YLE handbook (above) | The test's writing section offers a frame or picture prompt with a word count and a simple marking rubric (content / vocabulary / accuracy). |
| **L1 support** in rubrics lowers anxiety for beginners without replacing English. | General YL practice; Greek φροντιστήριο norm | Optional Greek help line under each task (on by default for A–C Senior), and Greek instructions on/off in the test builder. |

## Design rules we now follow
1. Short imperative rubrics in the YLE style; a worked example where the task type is new.
2. 5–8 items per task; mixed answer keys (never all True / all Yes / all A; correct choice position spread across a, b, c).
3. Only the unit's words are answers; forms are inflected to fit the sentence and word banks show the forms to write.
4. Pictures are the app's illustrations (`assets/pics`, same trusted lookup as the arcade), never emoji.
5. Each skill has recognition → recall progression; writing is scaffolded; listening uses new short texts (not the per-word recordings).
6. Level defaults: sentence length and item counts follow the class profile (A Senior ≈ 7 words per sentence … E Senior ≈ 16).

## Test Maker exercise library (2026-10-20)

The test's exercise picker became a library of 37 task types (6 skills). Each task carries what the children do, why (what it trains), its kind (**recognise → recall → create**), the classes it suits (A–E fit range) and a small paper preview. Rationale per addition:

| Addition | Why |
|---|---|
| **Kind badges + "This test" mix bar** (recognise / recall / create) | Retrieval and production build stronger memory than recognition alone (see retrieval and involvement-load rows above); a fair unit test moves from recognising to recalling to creating, so the teacher sees the balance at a glance. |
| **Class fit** ("Great for B Senior", "A stretch", "Easy") and per-class defaults | YLE task types change with the level (Starters picture-led → Flyers longer texts and own writing). Defaults follow that path: A/B start picture-led, C mixes, D writes an email, E an opinion paragraph. |
| **Reading:** who said it / questions / put the events in order / words in the text, plus 81 ready-made unit texts (fact, story, everyday text per unit of CPP 1–3) | Children need to *read connected text* that recycles the unit's words in a new context (involvement load: search + evaluation). Story texts suit sequencing; texts are 50–95 words in CPP1, 60–125 in CPP2, 85–160 in CPP3, in line with YLE reading lengths. |
| **Listening:** match people to things, complete the notes; plays once/twice; dialogue or monologue | YLE "listen and draw lines" and Flyers/KET note completion; hearing it twice is the YLE norm for young learners, once is a stretch for E Senior. |
| **Word families from A Senior** (class ladder: plural -s and -er/-y endings in A → un-/-ful/-ly in B–C → -ness/-tion/-able in D → two changes in E; the base word printed inside the answer boxes, s u n _ _, for young classes) | Morphological awareness grows with vocabulary from the first years and helps young learners decode and spell new words; starting with transparent, frequent endings keeps it within reach. |
| **Vocabulary:** unscramble/spell, word from its meaning, which words go together (collocation), word forms | Spelling and form-meaning recall; collocation and word-family work are the next step after single-word recall (B1 Preliminary word formation). Letter hints (first letters / letter count / none) scaffold by class. |
| **Grammar:** make the question, text cloze, translate from Greek | Question forming is a known gap for Greek learners; a cloze tests grammar in connected text; short L1→L2 translation targets L1 transfer errors explicitly. |
| **Writing:** sentences from starters, email/message, story from pictures, opinion paragraph; support full/some/none | Scaffolding fades with level: frames + word box → prompts only → independent writing (Flyers 20+ words, A2 Key email 25+, B1 Preliminary story/opinion ~100). |
| **Speaking:** tell the story, discuss a question | YLE picture-story and Key/Preliminary discussion tasks. |

Part settings (text length, text type, recording, plays, options, hints, support, difficulty) live on each part and reach the AI brief; the class's defaults fill what the teacher leaves untouched.

_Limits: most of the classroom evidence on spacing and involvement load comes from small studies or older learners; we use it as guidance, not proof._
